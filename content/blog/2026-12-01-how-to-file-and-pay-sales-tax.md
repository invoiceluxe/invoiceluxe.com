Collecting sales tax is only half the job. You also have to report it and send it to the state on time. The process is similar everywhere, even though the forms and deadlines are different.

## The money belongs to the state

Sales tax you collect from customers is not your income. You are holding it for the state. Keep it separate from your regular money, for example in a savings account, so you are never short when the return is due.

## Step by step

1. **Register.** Get a sales tax permit in each state where you must collect tax. See [do I need a sales tax permit](/blog/do-i-need-a-sales-tax-permit/).
2. **Collect the right amount.** Use the correct rate for each sale. See [combined sales tax rate explained](/blog/combined-sales-tax-rate-explained/).
3. **Record every sale.** Keep invoices that show the tax. See [how to show sales tax on an invoice](/blog/how-to-show-sales-tax-on-an-invoice/).
4. **Find out your filing schedule.** The state assigns monthly, quarterly or annual filing, usually based on how much tax you collect.
5. **Prepare the return.** Report total sales, non-taxable sales, taxable sales and tax collected.
6. **File and pay** by the due date, usually through the state's online portal.
7. **Keep records** of the return and the payment.

## A worked example

For one month, a small shop reports:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Total sales | $18,500.00 |
| Non-taxable or exempt sales | $2,500.00 |
| Taxable sales | $16,000.00 |
| Tax rate | 7% |
| **Tax due** | **$1,120.00** |

16,000 × 0.07 = 1,120.00. Real returns may have more lines, such as local tax by jurisdiction and discounts for timely filing, depending on the state.

## Filing frequency

| Frequency | Typical for |
|---|---|
| Monthly | Higher tax collected |
| Quarterly | Medium |
| Annually | Very small amounts |

The state assigns your schedule, and it can change as your sales grow.

## File even if you made no sales

In most states you must still file a return when you collected no tax, often called a "zero return". Skipping it can cause penalties.

## If you sell in several states

Each state has its own return, due date and portal. Track sales by state, so you can fill in each return quickly. See [what is sales tax nexus](/blog/what-is-sales-tax-nexus/).

## Common mistakes

- Missing the due date, which can cause penalties and interest
- Spending collected tax before the return is due
- Using the wrong rate or the wrong period
- Forgetting use tax on purchases. See [sales tax vs use tax](/blog/sales-tax-vs-use-tax/).

## FAQ

### How often do I file sales tax?

It depends on your state and your sales volume. Most states assign monthly, quarterly or annual filing.

### Do I file if I had no sales?

Usually yes, with a zero return. Check your state's rule.

### How do I pay sales tax?

Most states let you file and pay online through their revenue department's website.

### Is this tax advice?

No. Forms and deadlines vary by state. Confirm them with your state or an accountant.
