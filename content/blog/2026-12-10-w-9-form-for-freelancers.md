If you work as a freelancer or independent contractor in the US, a client will sooner or later ask you for a **W-9**. Here is what it is, why they ask and how to handle it.

## What is a W-9?

Form W-9, "Request for Taxpayer Identification Number and Certification", is an IRS form. You fill it in and give it to the client who pays you. It tells them your legal name, business type, address and **taxpayer identification number (TIN)**, which is your Social Security number or your EIN.

You give the W-9 to the **client**. You do not send it to the IRS.

## Why clients ask for it

The client needs your details to report what they paid you. For many contractors, that report is a **Form 1099-NEC**, which the client files with the IRS and sends to you after the year ends. Reporting rules include a payment threshold, and the threshold has changed recently, so check the current IRS instructions for the year in question.

## W-9 vs 1099

| | W-9 | 1099-NEC |
|---|---|---|
| Who completes it | You (the contractor) | The client |
| Purpose | Gives your tax details | Reports what you were paid |
| When | Before or at the start of work | After the tax year ends |

## When to send a W-9

- **When a new client asks**, ideally before your first invoice
- **When your details change**, such as a new name, address or business type
- **Not to people who did not hire you.** Be careful with unexpected requests, because a W-9 contains sensitive data

## What to fill in

1. Your name as shown on your tax return
2. Business name, if you use one
3. Federal tax classification, such as individual or sole proprietor, or LLC
4. Address
5. TIN: SSN or EIN
6. Signature and date

If you have an EIN, using it instead of your SSN keeps your Social Security number private.

## How to share it safely

- Send it through a secure channel or an encrypted upload, not a plain email if you can avoid it
- Never put your SSN or EIN on an invoice
- Only send it to a client who has actually hired you
- Keep a copy for your records

## How it fits with invoicing

Send the W-9 once, then invoice as usual. ## Common mistakes on a W-9

- **Wrong name.** Use the name on your tax return. If you use a business name, enter it on the separate line.
- **Wrong tax classification.** Choose the box that matches how you file your taxes, such as individual or sole proprietor, or LLC with the correct tax class.
- **Missing signature or date.** An unsigned form can be rejected.
- **A typo in the number.** Check every digit of your SSN or EIN.

A mistake can delay payment, because the client needs a correct form before they can report what they paid you.

## What if you do not give one?

A client who does not have your taxpayer ID may be required to withhold tax from your payments at a flat rate, known as backup withholding. As of this writing, the rate is 24%. That money goes to the IRS and can be claimed on your tax return, but it reduces what you receive. Check the current rate and rules with the IRS.

## If you are not a US person

Some freelancers outside the US use a different form, such as a W-8BEN, which is for foreign individuals. Ask the client which form they need, because the right form depends on your tax status.

## Keep your records

Keep a copy of every W-9 you send and note the date and client. Update the form if your name, address or business type changes. Keep your invoices, because you will compare them with the totals on the forms you receive after the year ends.

## A simple routine

1. Prepare a completed W-9 with your current details.
2. Send it securely when a new client asks.
3. Save the confirmation.
4. In January, compare the 1099 forms you receive with your own income records.

## FAQ

### What is a W-9 used for?

To give a client your name, address and taxpayer ID so they can report payments to you.

### Do I send my W-9 to the IRS?

No. You send it to the client who requested it.

### Do I need to charge sales tax if I fill out a W-9?

They are unrelated. The W-9 is about reporting your income, and sales tax depends on what you sell. See [do freelancers charge sales tax](/blog/do-freelancers-charge-sales-tax/).

### Do I need a W-9 for every client?

Each client that pays you as a contractor will usually ask for one. You complete the same form for each.

### Can I use my EIN instead of my SSN on a W-9?

Yes, if you have an EIN. It keeps your Social Security number private.

### Is this tax advice?

No. It is a general guide. Ask a tax professional about your own situation and check the current IRS instructions.
