Discounts look simple, but small errors can cost a business money or confuse customers. Here are six mistakes to check for, with the correct method for each.

> **Correct discount formula:** sale price = original price × (1 − discount ÷ 100)

## 1. Adding stacked discounts together

20% off followed by another 10% off is not 30% off. The second discount applies to the already reduced price.

100 × 0.80 = 80, then 80 × 0.90 = **72**. The total discount is 28%. See [stacked discounts](/blog/stacked-discounts/).

## 2. Working backwards the wrong way

A jacket costs 80 after a 20% discount. The original price is not 80 + 20% = 96. It is 80 ÷ 0.80 = **100**. Read [find the original price before a discount](/blog/find-original-price-before-discount/).

## 3. Discounting the price with VAT already included

Normally the discount applies to the net price, and VAT is added to the reduced amount. On 200 net with 10% off and 20% VAT: 180 net, 36 VAT, **216** total. See [discount and sales tax together](/blog/discount-and-sales-tax-together/).

## 4. Confusing discount with markup

Reducing a price by 20% and increasing it by 20% are not opposites. A price of 100 raised 20% is 120, and 120 reduced by 20% is 96. See [markup vs discount](/blog/markup-vs-discount/).

## 5. Forgetting the effect on profit

A 10% discount can remove far more than 10% of your profit. If your margin is 30, a discount of 10 on a price of 100 cuts profit from 30 to 20, which is one third of your profit.

| Price | Cost | Profit | Profit after 10% discount |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 70 | 30 | 20 |

## 6. Rounding too early

Rounding each step can leave you a cent or two off, which adds up on large orders. Calculate with full precision and round the final amount.

## Quick reference

## FAQ

### How do I calculate 20% off a price?

Multiply the price by 0.80. For 80, that gives 64.

### Do two 10% discounts equal 20%?

No. They equal 19%: 100 × 0.90 × 0.90 = 81.

### Is the discount applied before or after tax?

Usually before. The tax is then calculated on the reduced price. Check the rules that apply to you.

### How do I find the discount percentage from two prices?

(Original − sale) ÷ original × 100. For 100 and 75, that is 25%.
