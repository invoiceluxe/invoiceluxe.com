December is the best time to tidy your billing. A few hours now saves you stress at tax time and helps you start January with a clean slate.

## 1. Send every invoice you can

- Bill finished work now, instead of waiting for January.
- Send invoices for work that is delivered but not billed.
- Check long projects for milestones you can invoice.

Clients' budgets often close at year-end, so send invoices early in the month.

## 2. Chase open invoices

1. List every unpaid invoice with its due date.
2. Send polite reminders for anything overdue. See [payment reminder email for an overdue invoice](/blog/payment-reminder-email-overdue-invoice/).
3. Apply your late fee policy where it was agreed. See [late payment fees and interest](/blog/late-payment-fees-and-interest/).

## 3. Reconcile payments

Match each payment in your bank account to an invoice. Mark invoices as paid, and note any partial payments or credits. If something was wrong, fix it properly. See [how to correct an invoice](/blog/how-to-correct-an-invoice-credit-note/).

## 4. Check your sales tax

If you collect sales tax:

- Make sure every return for the year is filed.
- Compare the tax on your invoices with what you reported.
- Review each state's sales against its nexus threshold. See [what is sales tax nexus](/blog/what-is-sales-tax-nexus/).
- Check for rate changes that start on January 1. See [combined sales tax rate explained](/blog/combined-sales-tax-rate-explained/).

If you buy for resale, make sure your certificates are current. See [resale certificate explained](/blog/resale-certificate-explained/).

## 5. Prepare for tax forms

- Keep a list of clients who paid you during the year.
- Make sure you have a W-9 from anyone you will pay as a contractor, and be ready to give yours when clients ask. See [W-9 form for freelancers](/blog/w-9-form-for-freelancers/).
- Expect 1099 forms from clients in early next year, and compare them with your records.

## 6. Archive your documents

Save PDF copies of invoices, receipts and contracts in one folder for the year, named in a consistent way. If you have many files, you can combine them into one PDF. See [how to merge PDF files](/blog/how-to-merge-pdf-files/).

## 7. Review prices and terms

- Decide whether to raise your rates for the new year.
- Update payment terms if clients paid late. See [invoice payment terms](/blog/invoice-payment-terms/).
- Think about deposits for new projects. See [how to ask for a deposit](/blog/how-to-ask-for-a-deposit/).

## 8. Set up January

- Decide whether to restart invoice numbers or continue the sequence. See [how to number invoices](/blog/how-to-number-invoices/).
- Update your invoice template with new details.
- Schedule recurring invoices. See [recurring invoices and retainers](/blog/recurring-invoices-and-retainers/).

## FAQ

### When should I send my last invoices of the year?

As early in December as you can. Many clients close budgets and process payments before the holidays.

### Do I need to keep invoices after the year ends?

Yes. Keep them as long as your tax authority or accountant advises, which is often several years.

### Should invoice numbers restart in January?

Either is fine. Choose one system and stay consistent.

### Is this tax advice?

No. It is a general checklist. Ask a tax professional about your own situation.
