A receipt is proof that a payment was made. It protects both the seller and the buyer, and it is often needed for accounting, returns and tax records.

> **A receipt confirms money received. An invoice asks for money to be paid.**

## What should a receipt include?

- **Your name or business name** and contact details
- **A receipt number** so each one is unique
- **The date of payment**
- **The buyer's name** (when relevant)
- **A description of what was paid for**, with quantities
- **The amount paid**, including any tax shown separately
- **The payment method**, such as cash, card or bank transfer
- **A note that the payment was received**, for example "Paid in full"

## Receipt vs invoice: what is the difference?

| | Invoice | Receipt |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Request payment | Confirm payment |
| Sent | Before payment | After payment |
| Has a due date | Yes | No |
| Status | Unpaid | Paid |

Read [what to include on an invoice](/blog/what-to-include-on-an-invoice/) for the invoice side, and [how to send an invoice](/blog/how-to-send-an-invoice-to-a-customer/).

## When should you give a receipt?

Immediately after the payment is received. For cash payments it is especially important, since there is no bank record. Keep a copy of every receipt you issue.

## Do you need a receipt if you already sent an invoice?

Not always. A paid invoice can serve as proof, but many customers still like a receipt. You can mark the invoice "paid" with the payment date, or issue a separate receipt. Rules differ by country, so check what applies to you.

## FAQ

### Is a receipt legally required?

It depends on your country and the type of sale. Many places require one on request. Check your local rules.

### Can I write a receipt by hand?

Yes, as long as it contains the necessary details and is clear. A digital receipt is easier to store and search.

### Should a receipt show tax?

If you charge tax, show it separately so the buyer can see the net amount and the tax.

### How long should I keep receipts?

Record keeping periods vary by country, often several years. Ask your tax authority or accountant.
