Sales tax nexus is the connection between your business and a state that gives that state the right to make you collect and pay sales tax. If you sell online or ship to other states, nexus is the first thing to understand.

## What does nexus mean?

When you have nexus in a state, you must register there, charge the right sales tax and file returns. When you do not, you generally do not collect that state's tax.

There are two main types: **physical nexus** and **economic nexus**.

## Physical nexus

You have physical nexus when you have a real presence in a state. Common triggers include:

- An office, shop, warehouse or storage space
- Employees or independent sales reps working there
- Inventory stored there, including stock held at a fulfillment center
- Attending trade shows or pop-up events in some states

## Economic nexus

In 2018, the US Supreme Court decided *South Dakota v. Wayfair*. After that ruling, states can require out-of-state sellers to collect sales tax based on sales activity alone, even with no physical presence.

Most states set the threshold at **$100,000 in sales into the state** in the current or previous calendar year. Some also count **200 separate transactions**, though a growing number of states have dropped the transaction count.

| State | Commonly cited threshold |
|---|---|
| Most states | $100,000 in sales (some add 200 transactions) |
| California | $500,000 in sales |
| Texas | $500,000 in sales |
| New York | $500,000 in sales and more than 100 transactions |
| Alabama | $250,000 in sales |
| Mississippi | $250,000 in sales |

Thresholds change, so check your state's revenue department before relying on a number.

## A simple example

You run an online shop based in Ohio. This year you sell $120,000 of products to customers in a state with a $100,000 threshold. You have economic nexus there, even though you have never been to that state. In the same year you sell $60,000 to another state with the same threshold. You do not have nexus there yet, but you should track the total.

## What to do when you cross a threshold

1. **Register** for a sales tax permit in that state. See [do I need a sales tax permit](/blog/do-i-need-a-sales-tax-permit/).
2. **Collect** the correct rate from that point on. Rates may depend on the buyer's address. See [origin vs destination sales tax](/blog/origin-based-vs-destination-based-sales-tax/).
3. **File and pay** on the schedule the state gives you. See [how to file and pay sales tax](/blog/how-to-file-and-pay-sales-tax/).

## Marketplaces count too

If you sell on a platform such as Etsy, Amazon or eBay, the marketplace usually collects tax for those orders. In some states, those sales still count toward your own threshold. See [sales tax for marketplace sellers](/blog/sales-tax-for-etsy-amazon-ebay-sellers/).

## FAQ

### What is the difference between physical and economic nexus?

Physical nexus comes from having property, people or inventory in a state. Economic nexus comes from the amount or number of your sales into a state.

### What is the usual economic nexus threshold?

Many states use $100,000 in sales, and some also use 200 transactions. A few states, including California and Texas, use $500,000.

### Do I need to collect sales tax if I sell only a little out of state?

Not until you meet that state's nexus rules, but track your sales by state so you know when you are close.

### Is this tax advice?

No. It is a general overview. Rules differ by state and change often, so confirm with the state or a tax professional.
