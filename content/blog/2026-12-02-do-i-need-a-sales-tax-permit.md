Before you collect sales tax, you usually need a **sales tax permit** from your state. Without one, you generally cannot legally collect the tax, and you cannot file returns. Here is when you need one and how to get it.

## What is a sales tax permit?

It is a registration with your state's tax agency that allows you to collect sales tax and obliges you to file returns. Different states use different names:

- Seller's permit
- Sales and use tax permit
- Sales tax license
- Certificate of authority
- Retailer's license

They all do the same job: register you as a seller.

## Who needs one?

You generally need a permit if you:

- Sell taxable goods or services in a state
- Have **nexus** in a state through a location, employees, inventory or enough sales. See [what is sales tax nexus](/blog/what-is-sales-tax-nexus/)
- Sell at craft fairs, markets or pop-up events in a state that requires one
- Want to buy inventory tax-free with a [resale certificate](/blog/resale-certificate-explained/)

If you only sell non-taxable services, you may not need one. See [do freelancers charge sales tax](/blog/do-freelancers-charge-sales-tax/).

## Do not collect tax before you are registered

Collecting sales tax without a permit can cause problems. Register first, then add tax to your invoices.

## How to get a permit

1. **Find your state's tax agency.** This is usually the department of revenue or taxation.
2. **Gather your information:** business name and address, business type, owner details and an EIN or Social Security number as the form requires, and your expected sales.
3. **Apply online.** Most states offer an online application.
4. **Wait for approval.** It can be quick or take some days.
5. **Display or save the permit** as the state requires, and note your permit number.
6. **Learn your filing schedule.** See [how to file and pay sales tax](/blog/how-to-file-and-pay-sales-tax/).

## Cost

Many states do not charge a fee, and some charge a small fee or require a deposit. Check the application page for your state.

## What to do after you have it

- Add the **tax rate** to your invoices. See [how to show sales tax on an invoice](/blog/how-to-show-sales-tax-on-an-invoice/).
- Keep tax money **separate** from your regular funds.
- Set calendar reminders for **due dates**.
- Keep **exemption certificates** for customers who do not pay tax.

## What if you sell in several states?

You may need a separate permit for each state where you have nexus. Register as soon as you cross a threshold, and not after.

## A quick decision guide

Ask yourself these questions:

1. **Do I sell goods?** Most states require a permit for selling taxable goods.
2. **Do I sell taxable services?** Some states tax certain services. See [do freelancers charge sales tax](/blog/do-freelancers-charge-sales-tax/).
3. **Do I sell only online?** You still need a permit in a state where you have nexus. See [what is sales tax nexus](/blog/what-is-sales-tax-nexus/).
4. **Do I sell at events or pop-ups?** Some states require a temporary permit.
5. **Do I only sell through a marketplace that collects the tax?** You may not need to collect, but check whether you must still register.

If the answer to any of them is yes, look up your state's registration rules.

## What you need to register

Typical information includes:

- Your legal name and business structure
- Your business address and contact details
- A federal tax ID (EIN) or Social Security number, depending on your business type
- A description of what you sell
- Your expected sales
- The date you will start selling in the state

Have these ready before you start the online form, so you can finish in one sitting.

## Display the permit and use it correctly

Some states ask you to display the permit at your place of business. Keep the permit number handy, because suppliers may ask for it when you buy items for resale. See [resale certificate explained](/blog/resale-certificate-explained/) if you are not sure what to do with it.

## After you register

1. Set up the tax rate on your invoices or store.
2. Create a simple routine to track tax collected.
3. File returns on the schedule you are given. See [how to file and pay sales tax](/blog/how-to-file-and-pay-sales-tax/).

## If you closed or paused the business

Do not just stop filing. Tell the state, and close the account properly, or you may keep receiving notices and penalties.

## FAQ

### Do I need a sales tax permit to sell online?

If you must collect sales tax in a state, you generally need a permit there. Marketplace sales may be collected by the marketplace. See [sales tax for marketplace sellers](/blog/sales-tax-for-etsy-amazon-ebay-sellers/).

### Is a sales tax permit the same as a business license?

No. A business license allows you to operate. A sales tax permit allows you to collect and report sales tax.

### How long does it take to get a sales tax permit?

It depends on the state, from immediate online approval to a few weeks.

### Do I need a permit if I only sell a few items?

Possibly. Many states have no minimum for regular sellers. Occasional sales rules vary, so check.

### Can I charge sales tax before I get my permit?

No. Collecting tax without a permit is a problem in most states. Register first.

### Is this tax advice?

No. Requirements differ by state. Confirm them with your state's tax agency.
