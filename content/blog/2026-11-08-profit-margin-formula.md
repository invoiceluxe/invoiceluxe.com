Profit margin shows how much of each sale you keep as profit. Knowing your margin helps you price jobs, judge discounts and understand whether your business is healthy.

> **Profit margin = (price − cost) ÷ price × 100**

## How do you calculate profit margin?

Example: you sell a service for 100 and it costs you 60 to deliver.

1. Profit: 100 − 60 = 40
2. Divide by the price: 40 ÷ 100 = 0.40
3. Multiply by 100: **40% margin**

## What is markup and how is it different?

Markup is profit compared with **cost**, not with price.

> **Markup = (price − cost) ÷ cost × 100**

Using the same numbers: 40 ÷ 60 = 0.667, so the markup is **66.7%** while the margin is 40%. They are different measurements of the same profit.

| Markup | Margin |
|---|---|
| 25% | 20% |
| 50% | 33.3% |
| 100% | 50% |
| 200% | 66.7% |

A 50% markup does **not** give a 50% margin. This is one of the most common pricing mistakes. See also [markup vs discount](/blog/markup-vs-discount/).

## How do you set a price for a target margin?

Formula: **price = cost ÷ (1 − margin)**

Example: your cost is 70 and you want a 30% margin. 70 ÷ (1 − 0.30) = 70 ÷ 0.70 = **100**.

## How do you set a price with a markup?

Formula: **price = cost × (1 + markup)**

Example: cost 80 with a 50% markup gives 80 × 1.50 = **120**. The margin on that sale is 40 ÷ 120 = 33.3%.

## Why margin matters when you give discounts

A discount comes straight out of profit. On a price of 100 with a cost of 70, a 10% discount reduces profit from 30 to 20. Check your margin before you agree to a discount, and read [bulk discount pricing](/blog/bulk-discount-pricing/).

## Gross margin and net margin

- **Gross margin** counts only direct costs of the product or service.
- **Net margin** also subtracts overheads such as software, rent and tax.

## FAQ

### What is a good profit margin?

It depends on the industry. Services often have higher margins than retail. Compare yourself with others in your field.

### Is margin or markup bigger?

For the same profit, markup is always larger than margin, because cost is smaller than price.

### How do I turn margin into markup?

Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin). A 40% margin is 0.40 ÷ 0.60 = 66.7% markup.

### Is this financial advice?

No. It explains the calculations. For decisions about your business, talk to an accountant.
