Five US states do not charge a statewide sales tax: **Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon**. That sounds simple, but each one has a catch, and being based in one of them does not remove every obligation.

## The five states and what to know

| State | Statewide sales tax | The catch |
|---|---|---|
| Alaska | None | Some cities and boroughs charge local sales tax |
| Delaware | None | Businesses pay a gross receipts tax instead |
| Montana | None | Some resort areas charge local taxes |
| New Hampshire | None | Special taxes apply, such as on meals and rooms |
| Oregon | None | Large businesses may pay a corporate activity tax |

### Alaska

There is no state sales tax, but many local governments add their own. Alaska also has a code for remote sellers that applies to local taxes, with its own threshold.

### Delaware

Delaware does not tax the buyer at checkout. Instead, businesses pay a **gross receipts tax** on what they earn. The cost can show up in the prices you pay.

### Montana

There is no general state sales tax, though a few resort communities add local taxes.

### New Hampshire

No general sales tax. The state has other taxes, such as a tax on meals and rooms.

### Oregon

No sales tax at the register. Businesses above a certain level of Oregon sales may owe a separate corporate activity tax.

## Does that mean sellers never collect tax?

No. Three situations still matter:

1. **You sell to customers in other states.** If you have nexus there, you must collect that state's tax. See [what is sales tax nexus](/blog/what-is-sales-tax-nexus/).
2. **You buy from out-of-state sellers.** Your own state does not tax the purchase, but you may owe tax elsewhere if you ship goods across state lines.
3. **Local taxes apply.** In Alaska, local rules can still require collection.

## Example: shipping from Oregon to another state

Say you run a shop in Oregon and sell a $100 item to a buyer in a state with a 6.5% rate (the buyer's state is made up for this example). If you have nexus there, you collect 100 × 0.065 = **$6.50** and send it to that state. If you do not have nexus, you collect nothing, even though the buyer might owe use tax. See [sales tax vs use tax](/blog/sales-tax-vs-use-tax/).

## Should you move your business to a no-sales-tax state?

Sales tax is only one cost. Income tax, property tax, business taxes and where your customers live all matter. A seller in Oregon with customers in 40 states still has to follow those states' nexus rules. A tax professional can compare the real impact for your situation.

## What "no sales tax" means for different people

**As a shopper**, you pay no state sales tax on most purchases in these states. Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon have none statewide, and Alaska has none at the state level, though many Alaskan localities add their own.

**As a seller in one of these states**, you generally do not collect state sales tax on sales to local customers. You may still have to collect tax on sales to buyers in other states if you have nexus there. See [what is sales tax nexus](/blog/what-is-sales-tax-nexus/).

**As a seller shipping into one of these states**, you usually collect nothing for the state, but check Alaska, where local sales taxes may apply.

## Why prices can still be higher

States without sales tax need revenue from somewhere. Typical substitutes are:

- Income or property taxes
- Gross receipts taxes, such as Delaware's
- Special taxes on certain goods and services, such as New Hampshire's tax on meals and rooms
- Higher fees and local charges

A shopper may save money at the register but pay more elsewhere.

## Invoicing in a no-sales-tax state

If you are based in one of these states and sell only locally, a simple invoice with no tax line is enough. If you sell out of state, you may need to add tax for certain customers. A template with an editable tax rate field covers both cases. See [how to show sales tax on an invoice](/blog/how-to-show-sales-tax-on-an-invoice/).

## Checking the current rules

Tax laws change, and local rules matter, especially in Alaska. Check your state's revenue department site for the latest information, and ask an accountant before you decide where to base a business.

## FAQ

### Which states have no sales tax?

Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon do not have a statewide sales tax.

### Do I charge tax if I live in a no-sales-tax state?

Not on sales within your state, apart from any local taxes. Sales to buyers in other states can still require collection if you have nexus there.

### Do no-sales-tax states have other taxes?

Yes. Examples include gross receipts taxes, special taxes on certain goods and income taxes, depending on the state.

### Is it cheaper to shop in a no-sales-tax state?

At the register, often yes. But other costs, such as higher prices or other taxes, can reduce the savings.

### Do I need a sales tax permit in a no-sales-tax state?

Usually not for local sales. You may need one in other states where you have nexus.

### Is this tax advice?

No. It is a general overview, and state rules change. Confirm with the relevant state agency or a professional.
