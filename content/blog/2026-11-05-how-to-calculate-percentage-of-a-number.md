Percentages appear in prices, discounts, tax, tips and invoices. Once you know three simple formulas, you can solve almost every percentage problem.

> **Percentage of a number = number × percentage ÷ 100**

## How do you calculate a percentage of a number?

Example: what is 15% of 240?

1. Convert the percentage to a decimal: 15 ÷ 100 = 0.15
2. Multiply: 240 × 0.15 = **36**

So 15% of 240 is 36.

## What percent is one number of another?

Formula: **part ÷ whole × 100**.

Example: 45 is what percent of 180? 45 ÷ 180 = 0.25, and 0.25 × 100 = **25%**.

## How do you find the total when you know the percentage?

Formula: **part ÷ percentage × 100**.

Example: 30 is 12% of a number. 30 ÷ 12 × 100 = **250**.

## How do you add or subtract a percentage?

- Add 20%: multiply by 1.20. For example 80 × 1.20 = 96.
- Subtract 20%: multiply by 0.80. For example 80 × 0.80 = 64.

This is exactly how VAT and discounts work. See [how to calculate discount percentage](/blog/how-to-calculate-discount-percentage/) and [how to calculate VAT](/blog/how-to-calculate-vat/).

## Common percentage mistakes

- **Forgetting to divide by 100.** 15% is 0.15, not 15.
- **Mixing up the part and the whole.** The whole is the number you compare against.
- **Adding and subtracting the same percent.** +20% then −20% does not return to the start. See [percentage increase and decrease](/blog/percentage-increase-and-decrease/).

## FAQ

### What is 10 percent of a number?

Divide the number by 10. For 250, 10% is 25.

### How do I calculate 20 percent of a price?

Multiply the price by 0.20, or find 10% and double it.

### How do I turn a percentage into a decimal?

Divide by 100. For example 7.5% = 0.075.

### What is the difference between a percentage and a percentage point?

If a rate goes from 4% to 6%, it rose by 2 percentage points, which is a 50% increase.
