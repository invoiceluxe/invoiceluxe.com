VAT mistakes are easy to make and easy to miss, because the numbers often look almost right. These seven are the most common. Check your own invoices against them.

> **Quick test:** if you remove VAT by subtracting a percentage of the total, you are probably making mistake number 1.

## 1. Subtracting 20% instead of dividing by 1.20

A price including 20% VAT is £120. Many people take 20% off: 120 − 24 = £96. That is wrong. The correct net price is 120 ÷ 1.20 = **£100**. The VAT is £20. See [how to calculate VAT backwards](/blog/calculate-vat-backwards/).

## 2. Calculating VAT on the gross price

VAT is calculated on the net price. If an item costs £100 before VAT, the VAT at 20% is £20. Applying 20% to a price that already includes VAT gives £24, which is too much.

## 3. Mixing up VAT rates

Some items are standard rated, some reduced and some zero rated. Using one rate for everything on an invoice overcharges or undercharges. Check the rate for each line.

## 4. Applying a discount after VAT

Normally you discount the net price first and then add VAT. On £200 net with a 10% discount and 20% VAT: 200 − 20 = 180, VAT 36, total **£216**. If you add VAT first and then discount, the total can look the same, but the VAT amount shown on the invoice will be wrong. Read [discount and sales tax together](/blog/discount-and-sales-tax-together/).

## 5. Inconsistent rounding

Rounding each line and then adding gives a slightly different total than adding the lines and rounding once. Pick one method, apply it consistently and make sure the invoice adds up.

| Method | Example (3 lines of £3.33 net at 20%) |
|---|---|
| Round each line | 3 × £4.00 = £12.00 |
| Round the total | £9.99 × 1.20 = £11.99 |

## 6. Leaving out required details

A VAT invoice normally needs your VAT number, the customer details where required, the date, a description and the VAT amount. See [what to include on a VAT invoice](/blog/what-to-include-on-a-vat-invoice/).

## 7. Charging VAT when you should not (or the reverse)

Rules for cross-border sales, exemptions and registration thresholds vary. If you are unsure whether to charge VAT, ask your tax authority or an accountant rather than guessing.

## FAQ

### What is the most common VAT mistake?

Removing VAT by subtracting the percentage of the gross price instead of dividing by 1 plus the rate.

### How do I calculate 20% VAT correctly?

Net × 0.20 is the VAT, and net × 1.20 is the gross price.

### Is VAT calculated before or after a discount?

Normally after the discount, on the reduced net price. Check the rules that apply to your situation.

### Is this tax advice?

No. It explains common calculation errors. For your own VAT obligations, check with your tax authority or an accountant.
