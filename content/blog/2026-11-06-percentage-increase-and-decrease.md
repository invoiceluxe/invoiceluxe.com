Percent change tells you how much a number went up or down compared with where it started. You use it for price rises, sales growth, discounts and savings.

> **Percent change = (new value − old value) ÷ old value × 100**

A positive result is an increase. A negative result is a decrease.

## How do you calculate percentage increase?

Example: a price rises from 80 to 100.

1. Subtract: 100 − 80 = 20
2. Divide by the old value: 20 ÷ 80 = 0.25
3. Multiply by 100: **+25%**

## How do you calculate percentage decrease?

Example: a price falls from 120 to 90.

1. Subtract: 90 − 120 = −30
2. Divide by the old value: −30 ÷ 120 = −0.25
3. Multiply by 100: **−25%**

## Why does +20% then −20% not return to the start?

Because the second percentage is calculated on a different number.

Start at 100. Add 20%: 120. Take away 20% of 120, which is 24: **96**. You ended 4% below where you began.

| Change | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| +25% | 80 | 100 |
| −20% | 100 | 80 |
| +50% | 40 | 60 |
| −33.3% | 60 | 40 |

To undo a rise you need a smaller percentage drop, and to undo a drop you need a larger percentage rise. A fall of 20% needs a rise of 25% to recover.

## How do you apply a percentage change to a price?

- Increase by 15%: price × 1.15
- Decrease by 15%: price × 0.85

Example: 200 up 15% is 230. Then 230 down 15% is 195.50, not 200. See [find the original price before a discount](/blog/find-original-price-before-discount/) to work backwards.

## Percent change vs percentage points

If a rate moves from 4% to 6%, it rose by **2 percentage points**, which is a **50% increase** (2 ÷ 4). Mixing these up is a very common reporting mistake.

## FAQ

### What is the formula for percentage increase?

(New − old) ÷ old × 100.

### How do I calculate a 10 percent increase?

Multiply the number by 1.10.

### How do I calculate a 10 percent decrease?

Multiply the number by 0.90.

### What if the old value is zero?

Percent change cannot be calculated from zero because you cannot divide by zero. Use the absolute change instead.
