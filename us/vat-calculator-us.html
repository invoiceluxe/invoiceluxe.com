Free sales tax calculator: how it works

This sales tax calculator does two jobs. It adds sales tax to a price so you know what a customer will pay, and it removes sales tax from a total so you can see the real pre-tax price. It also works as a VAT calculator, because adding or removing a percentage tax uses the same math whether you call it sales tax, VAT, GST or HST.

Freelancers, small business owners, shoppers and bookkeepers use it to check receipts, price products, write invoices and reconcile books. Nothing is uploaded: every calculation happens in your browser.

How to calculate sales tax (formula)

You need two numbers: the price before tax and the tax rate.

  • Sales tax amount: tax = price × (rate ÷ 100)
  • Total price: total = price + tax, or total = price × (1 + rate ÷ 100)

Example: a $250.00 order with 7% sales tax. Tax is 250 × 0.07 = $17.50, so the total is $267.50.

How to remove sales tax from a total (reverse sales tax)

If a receipt shows only the total, divide by one plus the rate:

  • Net price: net = total ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100)
  • Tax included: tax = total − net

Example: you paid $108.25 and the rate is 8.25%. Net is 108.25 ÷ 1.0825 = $100.00, and the tax was $8.25.

Common mistake: do not subtract 8.25% of the total. 8.25% of $108.25 is $8.93, which gives $99.32, not $100.00. The tax was charged on the smaller pre-tax price, so you must divide.

How to find the sales tax rate from price and total

Use the Find Tax Rate tool above, or do it by hand: rate = (total − net) ÷ net × 100. An $80.00 item that costs $86.40 at checkout was taxed at (86.40 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = 8%.

Sales tax vs VAT: what is the difference?

Sales tax is collected once, at the final sale to the consumer. VAT (value-added tax) is charged at each stage of production and distribution, and businesses reclaim the VAT they paid on their own purchases. The US uses sales tax; the UK, EU and many other countries use VAT. Canada uses GST, plus provincial taxes (PST, QST) or a combined HST depending on the province. For a shopper or an invoice, the arithmetic of adding or removing the tax is identical.

Sales tax tips for freelancers and small businesses

  • Taxability varies. Many states do not tax most services, but some tax specific services, and digital goods and software are taxable in a number of states.
  • Economic nexus. Since the 2018 US Supreme Court decision in South Dakota v. Wayfair, states can require out-of-state sellers to collect sales tax once they pass a sales or transaction threshold. Thresholds differ by state.
  • Show tax on the invoice. List the pre-tax subtotal, the tax rate, the tax amount and the total as separate lines.
  • Resale and exempt buyers. Keep a valid resale or exemption certificate on file when you do not charge tax.
  • Keep records. Save invoices and receipts so you can file and reconcile accurately.

This tool gives arithmetic, not tax advice. Ask a qualified accountant about your specific obligations.

US sales tax explained: state, county, city and district taxes

The United States does not impose a general federal retail sales tax. Instead, sales tax is mainly administered at state and local levels. Depending on the address and the product or service, a checkout rate may combine a state rate with county, city, transit, or special-district taxes. That is why a statewide headline rate is not always the rate that belongs on a receipt.

Why the delivery or store address matters

For many transactions, sourcing rules determine which jurisdiction's tax applies. In-store purchases, shipped orders, marketplace sales, and services can follow different sourcing rules. A seller may need to identify the correct destination, determine whether the item is taxable there, and then apply the combined rate. Use the custom-rate field for the verified combined rate rather than assuming the state base rate is the final answer.

Economic nexus and remote sellers

A business without a physical location in a state may still have a sales-tax collection obligation after it crosses that state's economic-nexus threshold. Thresholds, measurement periods, transaction-count rules, registration requirements, and marketplace-facilitator rules differ by state and can change. A calculator can calculate the amount once the correct rate and taxability are known; it cannot decide whether your business has nexus or must register.

Taxable products, services and exemptions

States do not all tax the same things. Physical goods, prepared food, clothing, software, downloads, subscriptions, repair work, professional services, and digital services may receive different treatment. Resale purchases, qualifying nonprofit purchases, manufacturing inputs, and other exemptions generally require the right documentation. Confirm product taxability and exemption rules with the relevant state revenue department before invoicing.

US example: combined rate

If a taxable item costs $250 before tax and the verified combined state-and-local rate is 8.875%, the tax is $22.19 after rounding to cents and the customer total is $272.19. This is an arithmetic illustration, not a claim that 8.875% applies to every address in a particular state.

Global VAT, GST and consumption-tax guide

Outside the United States, consumption taxes are commonly called value-added tax (VAT) or goods and services tax (GST). Some countries also use provincial, state, regional, or municipal layers. The label alone does not determine the legal treatment: registration thresholds, place-of-supply rules, exemptions, reduced rates, zero-rating, invoice requirements, and recovery of input tax all depend on local law.

United Kingdom VAT

UK VAT-registered businesses generally account for VAT on taxable supplies and may recover eligible VAT on business purchases, subject to the rules. The UK has standard, reduced, zero and exempt treatments; zero-rated and exempt are not interchangeable. For a VAT-inclusive price, divide by 1 plus the applicable rate to extract the net amount. For a VAT-exclusive price, multiply by 1 plus the rate to obtain the gross amount.

European Union VAT

EU countries operate within a shared VAT framework but set their own standard and reduced rates within EU rules. Cross-border B2B and B2C sales can involve place-of-supply rules, reverse charge, distance-selling rules, and the One Stop Shop (OSS) system. The customer's country, customer status, product category, and transaction type may change the VAT treatment. Do not use one country's rate as a universal EU rate.

Canada: GST, HST, PST and QST

Canada uses federal GST and, in participating provinces, harmonized HST. Other provinces may have a separate provincial sales tax (PST), while Quebec administers QST. Whether a transaction uses GST alone, HST, or a combination of federal and provincial taxes depends on the province and the supply. This calculator can add a single combined percentage; for separate tax components or special provincial rules, calculate each component according to the applicable official guidance.

Australia, New Zealand, India and other GST systems

Australia and New Zealand use GST systems, while India uses a multi-component GST structure that can include central, state, integrated and cess components depending on the supply. Other jurisdictions use VAT, GST, sales tax, turnover tax, or retail transaction taxes with their own registration and reporting rules. Enter the rate that applies to the specific transaction and treat the result as a calculation aid—not a determination of legal liability.

VAT-inclusive vs VAT-exclusive prices

A VAT-exclusive or tax-exclusive price is the amount before tax. A VAT-inclusive or tax-inclusive price already contains the tax. This distinction matters when preparing quotations, comparing supplier prices, checking receipts, and displaying consumer prices. If a customer-facing price is tax-inclusive, extracting tax requires division; subtracting the rate from the gross amount gives the wrong answer.

  • Exclusive to inclusive: gross = net × (1 + rate ÷ 100).
  • Inclusive to exclusive: net = gross ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100).
  • Tax portion of an inclusive amount: gross − net.

For example, at 20% VAT, a net price of £100 becomes £120 gross. If £120 is already VAT-inclusive, the net is £100 and the VAT portion is £20—not £24.

How rounding affects invoices and receipts

Tax authorities and accounting systems may specify whether rounding happens per line, per unit, per tax rate, or at invoice level. Those approaches can produce small differences on invoices with many items. This calculator displays currency amounts to two decimal places for practical estimates. For filing, point-of-sale reconciliation, or legally compliant invoicing, follow the rounding method required in your jurisdiction and the rules used by your accounting software.

Sales tax calculator FAQ

How do I calculate sales tax?

Multiply the pre-tax price by the rate as a decimal and add the result to the price. $100 at 8.25% is $8.25 tax, for a total of $108.25.

How do I remove sales tax from a total?

Divide the total by 1 plus the rate as a decimal. $108.25 ÷ 1.0825 = $100.00. Do not simply subtract the percentage from the total.

How do I find the sales tax rate if I know the price and the total?

Subtract the price from the total, divide by the price, and multiply by 100. An $80.00 item that costs $86.40 was taxed at 8%.

Is there a federal sales tax in the United States?

No. Sales tax is set by states, and many cities and counties add local rates on top.

Which states have no statewide sales tax?

Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon. Alaska allows local sales taxes in some areas.

What is the difference between VAT and sales tax?

Sales tax is charged once at the final sale. VAT is charged at every stage and businesses reclaim the VAT they paid. The math for adding or removing the tax is the same.

Do freelancers have to charge sales tax?

It depends on your state and what you sell. Many states do not tax most services, but some do, and digital products are taxed in many states. Check with your state revenue department or an accountant.

Can I use this as a VAT, GST, HST, PST or QST calculator?

Yes. Enter the applicable percentage and select a currency. The arithmetic works for VAT, GST, HST and many percentage-based consumption taxes. It does not determine which tax applies or calculate complex multi-component rules automatically.

Does the calculator know my exact US city or county sales tax?

No. US rates can vary by street address, product type and transaction. Use the verified combined rate for the transaction; the state preset is only a base statewide reference.

Does it calculate tax for every country automatically?

No. It is a global percentage calculator, not a live tax-rate lookup service. Enter the rate confirmed for the relevant country, region, product and transaction date.

Does the result update automatically?

Yes. The net amount, tax and total update as soon as you edit the amount, rate, mode or currency. No Calculate button or account is required.
